# Pinned person interaction study

[Open the study](pinned-person-study.html) through the local Vite server at `/docs/design-concepts/2026-09-17/pinned-person-study.html`. This is a prototype using current runtime data, not a replacement for the visitor app.

The study opens with no person selected. The overview gives only collection-level facts, and People shows all 111 inductees without a preselected portrait. Once someone is chosen, that selection carries across People, Links, and Years. In People, their portrait and name occupy one fixed region and their photo is omitted from the adjacent grid. Tapping the portrait's `READ RECORD` band opens the complete source biography and record notes. In Links and Years, the fixed region becomes text-only so media appears once; Years offers a `RECORD` command there. The fixed region uses a concise honor summary; the complete source text remains in the record. Back from the first selection returns to the neutral overview. People has search but no alphabet menu.

With no person selected, Links presents an alphabetical, searchable relationship index. Choosing someone opens one force-directed portrait map. The selected portrait moves to a red frame at the center, and the fixed region becomes text-only so the image appears once. Strong ink-colored connections represent candidate archive person references; fine gray connections represent a derived shared class year. The header key distinguishes the two, without repeating category labels under every portrait. Tapping another portrait re-centers it and adds or removes people according to the new selection. The center opens the full record, and Back returns to the previous person or the neutral index. The map pans on narrow screens and honors reduced-motion settings.

Years is a continuous horizontal film timeline. Swiping, mouse dragging, wheel scrolling, and keyboard arrows move the film strip; large year targets and previous/next-year controls provide direct jumps. Every year from 2010-2026 has a position, including 2021 with no class record. The line initially rests at 2015, the first year with local films, without selecting any person or film. Tapping a film opens a fixed viewing area above the still-navigable line. Its tile becomes a text marker rather than repeating the video image. The viewing area has the matching film, English captions, and a draft transcript; on narrow screens the film and transcript are touch-switchable views. The selected person carries into People and Links. Years 2010-2014 have no local films in the media manifest. The 93 manifest films have caption and transcript files, but all are still marked for rights and text review; this study shows draft material for evaluation and does not change production kiosk approval rules. The default palette is near-white and black, with a dark option. Red remains a sparse signal for person focus. The study does not write visitor state or alter the production app.

## Design references

- [Cooper Hewitt's relationship exploration](https://labs.cooperhewitt.org/2017/new-relationships-a-summer-at-cooper-hewitt/) connects a selected person to related people while keeping the basis of the relationship legible. That informed the map's distinct archive and class-year edge treatments.
- [D3's force simulation documentation](https://d3js.org/d3-force/simulation) covers the tested force-directed layout engine used here. The study settles the simulation before animating between selections so portraits do not continually drift under a visitor's finger.
- [Cooper Hewitt's timeline study](https://labs.cooperhewitt.org/2013/a-timeline-of-event-horizons/) uses chronological context to invite investigation. The horizontal film line likewise keeps gaps visible as part of the experience.
- [New Museum Digital Archive chronology](https://archive.newmuseum.org/chronology) keeps years and archival records spatially connected. Here, the year rail and film strip remain visible while a selected film plays.
- [Cooper Hewitt's Dive into Color process](https://labs.cooperhewitt.org/2018/making-dive-into-color/) is a useful precedent for using collection attributes as the visual input and removing complex controls during prototyping.

## Audit notes

- Checked People, Links, and the full record from 320 x 640 through 3840 x 2160 during the earlier interaction audit. The map's dense 2010 cluster and representative people from every class year had no overlapping or out-of-bounds portrait labels. Rechecked the horizontal film line at 320 x 640, 390 x 844, 768 x 1024, 1440 x 900, and 3840 x 2160, including an open film. None produced page-width overflow; the 320 x 640 open-film view scrolls vertically rather than squeezing its player and strip.
- Tested all 17 year positions and the 2021 gap, all 93 film entries, touch swipe, mouse drag, wheel and keyboard navigation, year jumps, arrow controls, switching films while open, loaded draft transcripts, enabled English captions, and selection carryover through Links and People. Earlier audit coverage included map re-centering, changing connection sets, Back, full records, long names, and biography scrolling. The paragraph rendering preserves the complete biography text for all 111 people.
- Primary touch controls measure at least 44 CSS pixels in each direction. Light and dark text tokens were checked for contrast; this is not a claim of WCAG conformance. A physical 30-40 inch touchscreen and assistive-technology pass are still required.
- All 111 primary portrait paths exist. However, 53 primary files are under 300 pixels in at least one dimension, so a sharp large-format 4K portrait requires higher-resolution source media. The prototype does not invent pixels or replace approved images.
