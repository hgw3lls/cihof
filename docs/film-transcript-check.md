# Film transcripts and captions: what to look at

Checked 25 September 2026, across all 93 films on the display. Each film's
captions came from YouTube's automatic captions, and the transcripts were drawn
from those captions. All 93 are recorded as approved (21 September).

## Already fixed: the note to staff

Every transcript file opens with a note from the tool that made it: *"Draft
transcript generated from … Generated: … Review required before kiosk
approval."* The display was showing those lines to visitors above every
transcript. The note has been removed from all 93 files, and nothing else in
them changed; the decision is recorded in
`data/curation-decisions/transcripts-staff-note-2026-09-25.md`. The display
build now refuses any transcript that still opens with it.

Everything below changes approved words, so it is for you to decide.

## 1. Background music heard as "Heat"

Automatic captions often hear music as the word *heat*. It shows in both the
captions and the transcript of these films:

| Person | Film | "Heat" in the transcript | In the captions | What it looks like |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Branka Malinar | `BKuCcyGsdek` | 36 | 63 | About a tenth of its 420 words, in long runs: *"Heat. Heat. Oh, heat, heat. Heat. Yeah. Heat. Yeah."* |
| Raj Aggarwal | `DoZUzteeFMU` | 11 | 13 | 212 words for a 6-minute film: *"Now it's time to go. Heat. Heat. Heat. Heat. Heat. Heat."* |
| Svetlana Stolyarova | `ZT80-BrCIyk` | 10 | 16 | *"…fortunate to experience this program. Heat. Heat. Heat. Heat."* |
| Mayor Frank Jackson | `diVkkJkn0sY` | 8 | 12 | *"…as individuals and as families. Heat. Heat. Heat. Heat up here."* |
| Beverly Kerecman | `4jjbyRcwk3Y` | 3 | 7 | *"…music choice and Heat. Heat. My polish is here with…"* |
| David Gilbert | `XeheG0iGICs` | 2 | 2 | *"Ladies and gentlemen, David Heat. Heat."* |

Raj Aggarwal's film looks to be mostly music: its transcript is 212 words for
six minutes. The others are speech with music between.

**Choices:** remove the noise from those captions and transcripts, or replace it
with *[music]*. The caption timings stay the same either way. For Raj
Aggarwal's film a short transcript written by someone who has watched it may
serve a visitor better than what the captions caught.

## 2. One ceremony film standing for six people

These six 2024 inductees each have the same film: the whole 2024 ceremony,
`P34omi5XUiY`, 1 hour 44 minutes, with a 12,000-word transcript.

- Ambassador Edward F. Crawford
- Dona Brady
- Dr. Eugene Jordan
- Erika Puussaar
- Johnny K. Wu
- Veronica Dahlberg

A visitor who opens Dona Brady's film gets the ceremony from its beginning,
wherever her own part falls in it. **Choices:**
- keep it as it is;
- start each person's film at their own part (someone would need to note the
  times, and the display would need a small change to start a film part way
  in);
- leave the ceremony off these six until each person's part is cut.

## 3. Transcriber tags

`[BLANK_AUDIO]` is the transcriber saying it heard nothing. It is in:
- Berj Shakarian, `2KBxzdgiqJM` (captions and transcript);
- Carolyn Balogh, `qzHokEDkXQc` (captions and transcript).

It can simply come out.

The same caption also names her *"Carolyn Varo"* where the introduction says
Carolyn Balogh. Automatic captions mishear names like this throughout, so a
listen-through of each film is the only way to catch them all.

## Not a problem

Sound descriptions such as *[applause]*, *[cheering]*, *[clears throat]* and
*[speaking Spanish]* are normal in captions and help a visitor who cannot hear.
*[Halo music]* in Eugenia Stolarczyk's film is correct: the speaker asks for the
song *Halo* to be played.

## If you want the words corrected

Tell the developer which of the choices above to take. Corrections to captions
and transcripts can be made the same way biography corrections are: each one
shown, with who decided and when, before it reaches the display.
